📖 5.1 What is the Union Executive?

The Union Executive is the group of people who run the Government of India at the national level. Think of it like the "management team" of the country.

It consists of five key members who work together to govern India:

🏛️ The Five Members of the Union Executive
  • 👑 The President — Head of State (the formal head)
  • 🥈 The Vice-President — Second highest office
  • 🏛️ The Prime Minister — Head of Government (the real head)
  • 👥 The Council of Ministers — To aid and advise the President
  • ⚖️ The Attorney General of India — Chief legal advisor
IMPORTANT: Articles 52 to 78 of Part V of the Constitution deal with the Union Executive.
Union Executive = The team that runs the country. President is the formal head, PM is the real head, and others help them.

👑 5.2 The President of India (Articles 52 – 62)

🎓 Qualifications (Article 58)

To become the President of India, a person must meet five conditions:

  • ✅ Be a citizen of India
  • ✅ Have completed 35 years of age
  • ✅ Be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha
  • ✅ Not hold any office of profit under the Government of India or any State
  • ✅ Not be a member of Parliament or State Legislature (if so, must resign before contesting)

🗳️ Election of the President (Articles 54, 55)

The President is elected indirectly — meaning the common people do NOT vote directly for the President. Instead, an Electoral College votes.

🗳️ The Electoral College Consists Of:
  • ✅ Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (MPs)
  • ✅ Elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (MLAs)
  • ✅ Elected members of Legislative Assemblies of Union Territories (Delhi and Puducherry)
  • ❌ Nominated members (of Parliament and State Assemblies) do NOT participate

How is the voting done? The election uses proportional representation with the single transferable vote system. Voting is by secret ballot. Disputes about the election are decided by the Supreme Court (Article 71).

President is elected by MPs + MLAs (not by common people). Nominated members don't vote. Any dispute goes to the Supreme Court.

⚖️ Impeachment Process (Simple Explanation)

Impeachment is the process of removing the President for serious misconduct. Here's how it works:

1Charge initiated in either House of Parliament.

2First House must pass the charge by a 2/3 majority of its total membership.

3Other House investigates the charge.

4If the other House also passes it by 2/3 majority, the President is removed.

REMEMBER: Impeachment requires a 2/3 majority in BOTH Houses. It is a very serious process and has never happened in India's history.
President serves for 5 years. Can be removed only for violating the Constitution. Both Houses must pass the charge by 2/3 majority.

⚡ 5.2.4 Powers of the President (Part 1)

🏛️ Executive Powers

All executive actions of the Government of India are taken in the name of the President. The President appoints:

  • 👔 The Prime Minister and other ministers
  • ⚖️ The Attorney General, CAG, Chief Election Commissioner, and other Election Commissioners
  • 🏛️ The Governors of States
  • 👨‍⚖️ The Chief Justice and other judges of Supreme Court and High Courts
  • 🎖️ The Chiefs of Army, Navy, and Air Force

📜 Legislative Powers

  • 📢 Summons and prorogues Parliament
  • 🏛️ Dissolves the Lok Sabha
  • 🎭 Nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha (from literature, science, art, social service)
  • 🇮🇳 Nominates 2 members to the Lok Sabha (from Anglo-Indian community — expired in 2020)
  • ✍️ Gives assent to bills passed by Parliament. Can return a bill (except Money Bill) for reconsideration.
  • 📜 Promulgates Ordinances when Parliament is not in session (Article 123)
President appoints everyone important, opens and closes Parliament, and can pass emergency laws (ordinances) when Parliament is not in session.

💰 5.2.4 Powers of the President (Part 2)

💰 Financial Powers

  • 💵 Money Bills can be introduced in Parliament only with the President's recommendation
  • 📊 The Annual Financial Statement (Budget) is presented in Parliament in the President's name
  • 🏦 Appoints the Finance Commission every 5 years

⚖️ Judicial Powers

  • 👨‍⚖️ Appoints judges of Supreme Court and High Courts
  • 🕊️ Can pardon, reprieve, respite, remit, or commute sentences (Article 72)
  • ❤️ Pardoning Power: Can pardon death sentences and court-martial punishments

🚨 Emergency Powers

  • 🆘 National Emergency (Article 352)
  • 🏛️ President's Rule (Article 356)
  • 💸 Financial Emergency (Article 360)

🎖️ Military Powers

  • ⚔️ Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces
  • 🕊️ Can declare war or peace (subject to Parliament approval)

🌍 Diplomatic Powers

  • 🌐 Represents India internationally
  • 🤝 Appoints ambassadors and high commissioners
  • 🎩 Receives foreign diplomats
President controls money matters, can forgive criminals, can declare emergency, is the head of the armed forces, and represents India to the world.

🚫 5.2.5 Veto Powers of the President
Veto Type💬 Simple Meaning
Absolute VetoWithholds assent to a bill. The bill ends.
Suspensive VetoReturns a bill to Parliament for reconsideration. If Parliament passes it again with a simple majority, President must give assent. (Not applicable to Money Bills.)
Pocket VetoPresident keeps the bill pending indefinitely. No time limit.
Special VetoFor private members' bills.
Veto = President saying "no" to a bill. But he can't always say no — Parliament can override him for most bills.
