🗳️ 12.1 What is the Election Commission of India?

The Election Commission of India (ECI) is a constitutional body responsible for conducting free and fair elections in India. It is an independent body, not under the control of the executive.

Think of the ECI as the "referee" of Indian democracy — it ensures that elections are conducted fairly and that no one cheats the system.

📜 Constitutional Basis

Article 324: Superintendence, direction, and control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President shall be vested in the Election Commission.

ECI = The referee of Indian elections. It conducts free and fair elections under Article 324.

📜 12.4 Electoral Reforms in India

Electoral reforms are changes made to the electoral system to make it more free, fair, and transparent. They have evolved over decades to strengthen Indian democracy.

📅 12.4.1 Key Electoral Reforms (Before 2000)

YearReform
1950Election Commission established.
1961Election Commission made a multi-member body.
1988Anti-Defection Law (52nd Amendment) — disqualification of defectors.
1989Voting age reduced from 21 to 18 (61st Amendment).
199273rd and 74th Amendments — Panchayat and Municipal elections.
1996Model Code of Conduct made more stringent.

📅 12.4.2 Key Electoral Reforms (2000–2025)

YearReform
2002Election and Other Related Laws (Amendment) Act — Disclosure of criminal background, assets, and liabilities by candidates.
2003Representation of the People (Amendment) Act — Candidates with more than 2 children disqualified from Panchayat elections.
2004Election Commission's Guidelines — Candidates must declare their criminal antecedents.
2010Election Commission's Guidelines — NOTA (None of the Above) option introduced.
2013Supreme Court Judgment — Immediate disqualification of convicted legislators.
