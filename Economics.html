SECTION 1 — Basic Concepts of Economics

1What is Economics?

Economics is the study of how people, businesses, and governments produce, distribute, and consume goods and services.

Father of Economics: Adam Smith · Wrote "The Wealth of Nations" in 1776

Two Branches

BranchStudy Of
MicroeconomicsIndividual units (a consumer, a firm, a market)
MacroeconomicsWhole economy (national income, inflation, unemployment)
✦ Economics is about how we use limited resources to satisfy unlimited wants ✦

SECTION 2 — National Income

9What is National Income?

National Income is the total value of all goods and services produced by a country in a year. It is the most important indicator of a country's economic performance.

10National Income — Complete Data

TermFull FormMeaning
GDPGross Domestic ProductTotal value of goods and services produced within a country in a year
GNPGross National ProductGDP + Net income from abroad
NNPNet National ProductGNP – Depreciation
NDPNet Domestic ProductGDP – Depreciation
NINational IncomeNNP at Factor Cost
PCIPer Capita IncomeNational Income ÷ Population

11Key Formulas

  • GDP = C + I + G + (X – M)
  • C = Consumption · I = Investment · G = Government Spending · X = Exports · M = Imports
  • GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)
  • NNP = GNP – Depreciation
  • NNP at Factor Cost = NNP at Market Price – Indirect Taxes + Subsidies
  • National Income = NNP at Factor Cost
  • Per Capita Income = National Income ÷ Population

12GDP vs GNP — Simple Difference

ConceptSimple Meaning
GDPWhat is produced inside India
GNPWhat is produced by Indians (inside or outside India)
NNPGNP minus wear and tear of machinery
Per Capita IncomeAverage income per person

16Monetary Policy Tools

Repo Rate
Reverse Repo
CRR
SLR
Open Market
MSF
ToolMeaningEffect
Repo RateRate at which RBI lends to commercial banksIncrease → Loans become costly
Reverse Repo RateRate at which RBI borrows from commercial banksIncrease → Banks park money with RBI
CRRCash Reserve Ratio — % of deposits banks keep with RBIIncrease → Less money to lend
SLRStatutory Liquidity Ratio — % of deposits banks keep with themselvesIncrease → Less money to lend
Bank RateRate at which RBI lends long-termIncrease → Loans become costly
Open Market OperationsBuying and selling of government securitiesBuy → More money in economy
MSFMarginal Standing Facility — Emergency borrowingHigher than repo rate

Simple Explanation

  • Repo Rate: RBI lends to banks at this rate. If it increases, loans become expensive.
  • Reverse Repo Rate: RBI borrows from banks. If it increases, banks park money with RBI.
  • CRR: Banks must keep a percentage of deposits with RBI.
  • SLR: Banks must keep a percentage of deposits with themselves.
  • Bank Rate: Long-term lending rate.
  • Open Market Operations: RBI buys/sells government securities to control money supply.

Economic Planning is the process by which the government sets goals and allocates resources to achieve economic development. India adopted Five Year Plans from 1951 to 2017.

SECTION 5 — Budget

21What is a Budget?

A budget is a financial statement of the government's estimated receipts and expenditure for a financial year.

Financial Year in India: 1st April to 31st March

Types of Budget

TypeCondition
Balanced BudgetIncome = Expenditure
Surplus BudgetIncome > Expenditure
Deficit BudgetIncome < Expenditure

SECTION 6 — Economic Reforms

23What are Economic Reforms?

Economic Reforms are changes made to the economic policies of a country to improve its performance. India introduced major economic reforms in 1991.

24LPG Reforms (1991) — Complete Data

FeatureDetail
Year1991
Prime MinisterP.V. Narasimha Rao
Finance MinisterDr. Manmohan Singh
LPGLiberalization, Privatization, Globalization
PurposeTo solve balance of payments crisis
ResultEconomic growth, Foreign investment

Simple Explanation

  • Liberalization: Removing government restrictions on business.
  • Privatization: Selling government-owned companies to private owners.
  • Globalization: Integrating India's economy with the world economy.
Exam Focus: The 1991 LPG Reforms changed India's economy. They opened India to foreign investment and increased economic growth. Remember: P.V. Narasimha Rao (PM) and Dr. Manmohan Singh (FM).
