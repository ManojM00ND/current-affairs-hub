🏛️ Advent of Europeans MODERN INDIA — CHAPTER 1 Explained in Simple Language — One by One Part 1: Why Did Europeans Come to India? India was always famous for its wealth. It had spices, cotton, silk, indigo, and precious stones. European countries wanted these goods. But the land route to India was controlled by Arab traders and Turkish rulers. They charged heavy taxes. So, European countries started looking for a sea route to India. This led to the Age of Discovery. Imagine you want to buy spices from India. But the road is blocked by middlemen who charge you double. So, you try to find a new way — by sea. That is exactly what Europeans did. Why is this important? This was the beginning of European rule in India. It changed India's politics, economy, and culture. Vasco da Gama was a Portuguese explorer. He was the first European to reach India by sea. He landed at Calicut in 1498 AD. The ruler of Calicut was Zamorin. Francisco de Almeida was the first Portuguese Governor in India. Alfonso de Albuquerque was the second Portuguese Governor. He captured Goa in 1510. Goa became the Portuguese capital in India. The Portuguese controlled trade for almost a century. They were very powerful. The British came to India for trade. But slowly, they captured political power. They defeated the Nawabs and Kings one by one. By 1857, they controlled most of India. Why is this important? The British changed India's politics, economy, and society. They introduced new laws, new education, and new technology. But they also exploited India's wealth. It laid the foundation of British rule in India. Why is this important? This battle established British power in Bengal. It opened the door for British rule in India. Part 3: Battle of Buxar (1764) — One by One Feature Detail Year 1764 Place Buxar, Bihar British Commander Hector Munro Alliance Against British Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh), Shah Alam II (Mughal Emperor) Result British won. British Rule — Complete Guide Page 1 Part 4: Treaty of Allahabad (1765) — One by One Feature Detail Year 1765 Signatory Robert Clive and Shah Alam II Significance British got Diwani rights (right to collect taxes) in Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha. After the Battle of Buxar, the British signed the Treaty of Allahabad in 1765. The Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II gave the British the Diwani rights — the right to collect taxes in Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha. This made the British very rich. They collected taxes but did not take responsibility for administration. Why is this important? This treaty gave the British economic control over Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha. It started the dual government system. Part 5: Dual Government of Bengal (1765-1772) Feature Detail Period 1765 – 1772 Introduced By Robert Clive System British had Diwani (tax collection). Nawab had Nizamat (administration). Significance British had power without responsibility. The Dual Government was introduced by Robert Clive in 1765. In this system: The British had Diwani — the right to collect taxes. The Nawab had Nizamat — the responsibility for administration. The British collected taxes but did not take responsibility for administration. This was very profitable for the British. Part 6: Governor-Generals of India — 1. Lord Wellesley (1798 – 1805) Feature Detail Subsidiary Alliance Introduced Fourth Anglo-Mysore War 1799 Second Anglo-Maratha War 1803 – 1805 Significance Expanded British Empire through Subsidiary Alliance. Lord Wellesley introduced the Subsidiary Alliance. In this system: Indian rulers kept their army but paid the British for protection. They could not fight wars without British permission. They could not employ Europeans without British permission. This expanded British control over India. Why is this important? The Subsidiary Alliance made Indian rulers dependent on the British. Lord Dalhousie (1848 – 1856) Feature Detail Doctrine of Lapse Introduced Annexation of Satara 1848 Annexation of Jhansi 1853 Annexation of Awadh 1856 First Railway Line 1853 (Bombay to Thane) First Telegraph Line 1851 Significance Expanded British Empire through Doctrine of Lapse. Lord Dalhousie introduced the Doctrine of Lapse. In this system: If an Indian ruler died without a natural heir, his kingdom would lapse (go) to the British. He annexed Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), and Awadh (1856). He also introduced the first railway line (Bombay to Thane, 1853) and the first telegraph line (1851). Why is this important? The Doctrine of Lapse angered many Indian rulers. It was one of the causes of the Revolt of 1857. The Revolt of 1857 was a major uprising against British rule in India. It was the first time that Indians fought together against the British. It started on 10th May 1857 in Meerut. It spread to Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi, and other places. Why is it important? It was the first war of Indian independence. It shook the British Empire. It led to the end of the East India Company's rule. The British crown took over India directly. Cripps Mission (1942) Proposed Dominion Status. Rejected. Quit India Movement (1942) "Do or Die." British arrested leaders. Cabinet Mission (1946) Proposed united India. Accepted initially. Direct Action Day (1946) Massive riots. Partition inevitable. Mountbatten Plan (1947) Partition of India into India and Pakistan. Indian Independence Act, 1947: Passed by British Parliament on 18th July 1947. Partition of India into India and Pakistan. Both became independent on 15th August 1947. Radcliffe Line (1947): Drawn by Sir Cyril Radcliffe on 17th August 1947. Divided Punjab and Bengal. Caused massive migration and violence. Independence Day (15th August 1947): India became independent. First Prime Minister: Jawaharlal Nehru. First President: Dr. Rajendra Prasad. First Governor-General: Lord Mountbatten.
