CHAPTER 1: PRE-HISTORIC INDIA

Part 1: What Does "Pre-Historic" Mean?

The word "Pre-Historic" is made of two parts. Pre = Before. Historic = History (written records). So, Pre-Historic means the time before humans learned to write.

Why is this important? Because there is no written proof. We only know about this time from things they left behind — stone tools, bones, cave paintings, and pottery. Archaeologists dig the ground and find these things.

When was this time? From about 5,00,000 BC to 1,000 BC.

Part 2: The Four Phases of Pre-Historic India

Palaeolithic Age | 5,00,000 BC - 10,000 BC | Old Stone Age
Mesolithic Age | 10,000 BC - 6,000 BC | Middle Stone Age
Neolithic Age | 6,000 BC - 1,000 BC | New Stone Age
Chalcolithic Age | 3,000 BC - 500 BC | Copper-Stone Age

Part 3: Palaeolithic Age (Old Stone Age)

Time Period: 5,00,000 BC - 10,000 BC

Palaeo = Old. Lithic = Stone. Palaeolithic means Old Stone Age.

Tools: Hand Axe, Cleaver, Chopper. Material: quartzite, flint, limestone.

Occupation: Hunting, gathering, fishing. Society: Small nomadic groups. Shelter: Caves, rock shelters. Fire: Discovered in this period. Food: Raw meat, fruits, nuts, roots. Burial: No evidence of burial. Art: No art.

Important sites: Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh) - Famous for rock paintings. Soan Valley (Punjab, Pakistan) - Stone tools found. Narmada Valley (Madhya Pradesh) - Hand axes found.

Sub-Phases: Lower Palaeolithic (5,00,000 BC - 50,000 BC) - Hand axes, cleavers. Middle Palaeolithic (50,000 BC - 40,000 BC) - Smaller tools, scrapers. Upper Palaeolithic (40,000 BC - 10,000 BC) - Blade tools, burins.

Part 4: Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age)

Time Period: 10,000 BC - 6,000 BC. Meso = Middle. Lithic = Stone.

Tools: Microliths (small stone tools).

Occupation: Hunting, gathering, fishing. Society: Small nomadic groups. Shelter: Caves, rock shelters. Domestication: Started taming animals (dog, sheep, goat). Burial: Evidence of burial. Art: Rock paintings (Bhimbetka).

Important sites: Bhimbetka (MP) - Rock paintings. Langhnaj (Gujarat) - Microliths. Bagor (Rajasthan) - Microliths. Sarai Nahar Rai (UP) - Burial.

Part 5: Neolithic Age (New Stone Age)

Time Period: 6,000 BC - 1,000 BC. Neo = New. Lithic = Stone.

Tools: Polished Stone Tools, Celts, Adzes.

Occupation: Agriculture, animal domestication. Society: Settled villages. Shelter: Mud houses, pit houses. Crops: Wheat, barley, rice. Pottery: Yes. Burial: Evidence of burial.

Important sites: Mehrgarh (Balochistan) - Earliest evidence of agriculture. Burzahom (Kashmir) - Pit houses. Chirand (Bihar) - Pottery. Koldihwa (UP) - Rice husk.

Part 6: Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Age)

Time Period: 3,000 BC - 500 BC. Chalco = Copper. Lithic = Stone.

Tools: Copper Tools, Stone Tools.

Occupation: Agriculture. Society: Settled villages. Pottery: Yes. Burial: Evidence of burial.

Important sites: Inamgaon (Maharashtra) - Agriculture. Navdatoli (MP) - Pottery. Ahar (Rajasthan) - Copper tools.

CHAPTER 2: INDUS VALLEY CIVILIZATION

Part 1: What Was the Indus Valley Civilization?

The Indus Valley Civilization (IVC) is also called the Harappan Civilization because Harappa was the first city discovered.

Duration: About 3300 BC to 1300 BC. Mature Period: 2600 BC to 1900 BC.

Location: Mostly in Pakistan and North-West India (Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh).

Discovery: 1826 - Charles Masson first mentioned Harappa. 1921 - Harappa was excavated by Daya Ram Sahni. 1922 - Mohenjo-Daro was excavated by R.D. Banerjee.

Metals Used: Copper, bronze, gold, silver.
No Iron: Iron was NOT known.

Cotton: First civilization to produce cotton.

Part 7: Religion

Mother Goddess: Worshipped for fertility.
Pashupati: Three-faced male deity (proto-Shiva).
Nature Worship: Trees (Pipal), Animals (Bull, Unicorn).
Temples: No evidence.
Fire Altars: Found at Kalibangan and Lothal.
Unicorn: Most common animal on seals.

Part 8: Society

Matriarchal: Mother Goddess worshipped.
Caste System: No evidence.
Dress: Men - Dhoti and Shawl. Women - Skirts and Ornaments.
Ornaments: Gold, silver, copper.
Food: Wheat, barley, milk, dates, fruits, fish.
Games: Dice games.
No Weapons: Very few weapons found.

Part 9: Art and Craft

Bead Making: Center at Chanhudaro.
Pottery: Red and black pottery.
Seals: Steatite, terracotta, copper.
Bronze Casting: Lost wax technique.

CHAPTER 3: VEDIC PERIOD

Part 1: What Was the Vedic Period?

After the Indus Valley Civilization declined, a new culture emerged in India. This was the Vedic Period. It is called "Vedic" because the Vedas were composed during this time.

Duration: 1500 BC - 600 BC.

Location: Mainly in the Sapta Sindhu region (Land of Seven Rivers) - Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh.

Two Parts: Early Vedic Period (Rigvedic Period): 1500 BC - 1000 BC. Later Vedic Period: 1000 BC - 600 BC.

Part 2: The Four Vedas

Rigveda: 1028 hymns. Book of Hymns. Oldest Veda. Hymns to gods (Indra, Agni, Varuna).

Samaveda: 1875 hymns. Book of Melodies. Origin of Indian music.

Yajurveda: 1975 hymns. Book of Rituals. Guide for priests performing sacrifices.

Atharvaveda: 5977 hymns. Book of Spells. Charms and spells for daily life.

Part 3: Other Important Vedic Texts

Brahmanas: Explanations of rituals.
Aranyakas: Forest Texts.
Upanishads: Philosophical Texts.
Vedangas: Limbs of Vedas - Shiksha, Kalpa, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Chhanda, Jyotisha.
Upavedas: Subsidiary Vedas - Ayurveda, Dhanurveda, Gandharvaveda, Shilpaveda.
Puranas: Mythological texts.
Epics: Ramayana, Mahabharata.

Part 4: Early Vedic Period (1500-1000 BC)

Society: Tribal.
Political System: King (Rajan) was elected.
Economy: Pastoral (cattle rearing).
Religion: Nature worship (Indra, Agni, Varuna).
Women: Respectable position.
Caste System: Flexible.
Main God: Indra.
Cow: Most important animal.
Wars: Fought for cows (Gavishthi).

Part 5: Later Vedic Period (1000-600 BC)

Society: Varna system became rigid.
Political System: King became hereditary.
Economy: Agriculture became primary.
Religion: Rituals became complex.
Women: Position declined.
Caste System: Rigid.
Main God: Prajapati.
Iron: Used in this period.
Ashramas: Four stages of life.

Part 6: Vedic Society

Varna System: Brahmins (Priests), Kshatriyas (Warriors), Vaishyas (Traders), Shudras (Servants).

Ashramas: Brahmacharya (Student), Grihastha (Householder), Vanaprastha (Retired), Sannyasa (Renunciate).

Women: Early Vedic - Respectable. Later Vedic - Position declined.

Marriage: Monogamy was common. Polygamy was allowed for kings.

Education: Gurukul system. Oral transmission.

Part 7: Vedic Polity

King: Rajan.
Sabha: Council of elders.
Samiti: General assembly.
Vidatha: Oldest assembly.
Purohita: Chief priest.
Senani: Commander.
Gramani: Village headman.
Bali: Voluntary offering.
Bhaga: Share of produce.

Part 8: Vedic Economy

Early Vedic: Pastoral.
Later Vedic: Agriculture.
Crops: Wheat, barley, rice.
Animals: Cow, horse, sheep, goat.
Trade: Barter system.
Gavishthi: Search for cows.

Part 9: Vedic Religion

Early Vedic: Nature worship.
Later Vedic: Rituals became complex.
Indra: God of rain and war.
Agni: God of fire.
Varuna: God of water.
Prajapati: Creator God.
Yajna: Sacrifice.
No Idols: No idol worship.
No Temples: No temples.

Part 10: Important Terms

Rajan: King. Sabha: Council of elders. Samiti: General assembly. Vidatha: Oldest assembly. Purohita: Chief priest. Senani: Commander. Gramani: Village headman. Gavishthi: Search for cows. Bali: Voluntary offering. Bhaga: Share of produce. Yajna: Sacrifice. Varna: Caste. Ashrama: Stage of life. Gurukul: School.

CHAPTER 4: BUDDHISM AND JAINISM

Part 1: Why Did Buddhism and Jainism Start?

In the 6th century BC, India was going through big changes. The Vedic religion had become very complex. Priests (Brahmins) dominated society. Animal sacrifices were common. The caste system was rigid.

Two new religions emerged: Buddhism - founded by Gautama Buddha. Jainism - founded by Mahavira (24th Tirthankara).

These are called Heterodox Sects because they did not accept the authority of the Vedas.

Part 2: Buddhism - Basic Facts

Founder: Gautama Buddha (Siddhartha).
Born: 563 BC.
Birthplace: Lumbini (Nepal).
Father: Suddhodana (King of Kapilavastu).
Mother: Maya Devi.
Wife: Yashodhara.
Son: Rahul.
Enlightenment: 528 BC.
Place of Enlightenment: Bodh Gaya (Bihar).
First Sermon: 528 BC.
Place of First Sermon: Sarnath (UP).
Death (Mahaparinirvana): 483 BC.
Place of Death: Kushinagar (UP).
Tree of Enlightenment: Bodhi Tree (Pipal).
Symbol: Lotus.

Part 3: Buddhism - Four Noble Truths

1. Dukkha - Suffering exists.
2. Samudaya - Cause of suffering (desire).
3. Nirodha - Suffering can end.
4. Magga - Path to end suffering (Eightfold Path).

Part 4: Buddhism - Eightfold Path

1. Right View - Understanding the Four Noble Truths.
2. Right Intention - Thinking with love, not anger.
3. Right Speech - Speaking truth, not lying.
4. Right Action - Not killing, not stealing.
5. Right Livelihood - Earning honestly.
6. Right Effort - Working hard for good.
7. Right Mindfulness - Being aware of your body and mind.
8. Right Meditation - Focusing the mind.

Part 5: Buddhism - Triple Gem (Triratna)

Buddha - The Enlightened One.
Dhamma - The Teaching.
Sangha - The Community of Monks.

Part 6: Buddhism - Buddhist Councils

1st - 483 BC - Rajgriha - Ajatashatru - Mahakassapa
2nd - 383 BC - Vaishali - Kalashoka - Sabakami
3rd - 250 BC - Pataliputra - Ashoka - Moggaliputta Tissa
4th - 78 AD - Kundalvana (Kashmir) - Kanishka - Vasumitra

Part 7: Buddhism - Sects

Hinayana (Lesser Vehicle): Theravada. Buddha was a human. No idol worship.
Mahayana (Greater Vehicle): Buddha was a god. Idol worship. Bodhisattvas.

Part 8: Buddhism - Key Terms

Buddha: The Enlightened One.
Dhamma: The Teaching.
Sangha: The Community.
Bodhisattva: A being who seeks enlightenment.
Nirvana: Liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
Stupa: Dome-shaped structure containing relics.
Chaitya: Prayer hall.
Vihara: Monastery.
Pali: Language of Buddhist texts.

Part 9: Jainism - Basic Facts

Founder: Mahavira (Vardhamana).

Death: 468 BC.

Symbol: Lion.
Number: 24th Tirthankara.

Part 10: Jainism - Five Vows (Pancha Mahavrata)

1. Ahimsa - Non-violence.
2. Satya - Truth.
3. Asteya - Non-stealing.
4. Brahmacharya - Celibacy.
5. Aparigraha - Non-possession.

Part 11: Jainism - Triple Gem (Triratna)

Right Faith - Belief in Jain teachings.
Right Knowledge - Understanding Jain teachings.
Right Conduct - Following Jain teachings.

Part 12: Jainism - Sects

Digambara (Sky-clad): Monks do not wear clothes. Strict asceticism.
Svetambara (White-clad): Monks wear white clothes. More liberal.

Part 13: Jainism - Tirthankaras

2. Ajitanatha - Elephant

Part 14: Jainism - Key Terms

Kevala: Perfect knowledge.
Nirvana: Liberation.
Ahimsa: Non-violence.
Anekantavada: Many-sided reality.
Syadvada: Theory of maybe.
Sallekhana: Fasting unto death.

Part 15: Buddhism vs Jainism - Comparison

Founder: Gautama Buddha vs Mahavira.
Birth: 563 BC vs 540 BC.

Enlightenment: Bodh Gaya vs Jrimbhikagrama.

Symbol: Lotus vs Lion.
Main Teaching: Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path vs Five Vows, Triple Gem.
Sects: Hinayana, Mahayana vs Digambara, Svetambara.
Language: Pali vs Prakrit.

CHAPTER 5: MAURYAN EMPIRE

Part 1: What Was the Mauryan Empire?

The Mauryan Empire was the first pan-Indian empire. It was founded by Chandragupta Maurya in 321 BC.

Duration: 321 BC - 185 BC.
Founder: Chandragupta Maurya.
Greatest Ruler: Ashoka the Great.
Capital: Pataliputra (modern Patna).
Last Ruler: Brihadratha (killed by Pushyamitra Shunga in 185 BC).

Part 2: Chandragupta Maurya (321 BC - 297 BC)

Founder: Chandragupta Maurya.
Advisor: Chanakya (Kautilya).
Book: Arthashastra (written by Chanakya).
Greek Ambassador: Megasthenes.
Book by Megasthenes: Indica.
Religion: Jainism (later became a Jain monk).
Death: 297 BC (Sallekhana - fasting unto death).

Part 3: Ashoka the Great (268 BC - 232 BC)

Coronation: 268 BC.
Kalinga War: 261 BC.
Religion: Buddhism.
Dhamma: Ashoka's policy of moral law.
Inscriptions: 33 inscriptions (Rock Edicts, Pillar Edicts).
Language: Prakrit.
Script: Brahmi.
Symbols: Sarnath Lion Capital (National Emblem), Ashoka Chakra (National Flag).
Stupas: Sanchi Stupa, Sarnath Stupa.
Death: 232 BC.

Part 4: Mauryan Administration

Central: King was supreme. Council of Ministers (Mantriparishad).
Provincial: Empire divided into provinces. Governors (Kumaras) appointed.
Local: Village headman (Gramani).
Taxation: Land tax (Bhaga), Trade tax, Irrigation tax.
Army: Large standing army.
Spy System: Extensive spy network.

Part 5: Mauryan Economy

Agriculture: Main occupation. Wheat, barley, rice.
Trade: Internal and external.
External Trade: With Rome, Greece, and South-East Asia.
Currency: Coins (Panas) were used.
Roads: Built roads for trade and communication.
Irrigation: Canals and wells were built.

Part 6: Ashoka's Dhamma

Ahimsa: Non-violence.
Tolerance: Respect for all religions.
Obedience to Parents: Respect your father and mother.
Respect for Teachers: Respect your teachers.
Kindness to Servants: Be kind to your servants.
Charity: Give to the poor.

Part 7: Ashokan Inscriptions

Major Rock Edicts: 14 edicts. Deals with Dhamma.
Minor Rock Edicts: Personal messages of Ashoka.
Separate Rock Edicts: Found at Dhauli and Jaugada.
Major Pillar Edicts: 7 edicts. Deals with Dhamma.
Minor Pillar Edicts: Deals with Buddhist matters.

Part 8: Decline of Mauryan Empire

Weak Successors: After Ashoka, the rulers were weak.
Huna Invasions: Foreign invasions weakened the empire.
Feudal System: Land grants to officials made them powerful.
Economic Decline: Heavy taxes and corruption.
Spread of Buddhism: People became more peace-loving and less warlike.

In 185 BC, Brihadratha (the last Mauryan ruler) was killed by Pushyamitra Shunga.

CHAPTER 6: POST-MAURYAN PERIOD

Part 1: What Was the Post-Mauryan Period?

After the Mauryan Empire declined in 185 BC, India was divided into many small kingdoms. This period is called the Post-Mauryan Period. It lasted from 185 BC to 300 AD.

Main kingdoms: Shunga Dynasty (185 BC - 73 BC), Kushana Dynasty (1st Century AD - 3rd Century AD), Satavahana Dynasty (1st Century BC - 3rd Century AD), Sangam Age (300 BC - 300 AD).

Part 2: Shunga Dynasty (185 BC - 73 BC)

Founder: Pushyamitra Shunga.
Capital: Pataliputra.
Religion: Brahmanism.
Significance: Patronized Brahmanism. Patanjali wrote Mahabhashya.
Decline: Overthrown by the Kanvas.

Part 3: Kushana Dynasty (1st Century AD - 3rd Century AD)

Founder: Kujula Kadphises.
Greatest Ruler: Kanishka (78 AD - 101 AD).
Capital: Purushapura (Peshawar).
Religion: Buddhism.
Significance: Patronized Buddhism. 4th Buddhist Council at Kundalvana.
Silk Route: Controlled the Silk Route. Trade with Rome.
Court Poets: Ashvaghosha (Buddhacharita), Vasumitra.

Part 4: Satavahana Dynasty (1st Century BC - 3rd Century AD)

Founder: Simuka.
Capital: Pratishthana (Paithan).
Greatest Ruler: Gautamiputra Satakarni.
Religion: Buddhism.
Significance: Patronized Buddhism. Land grants to Brahmins started.
Trade: With Rome. Roman coins found in South India.

Part 5: Sangam Age (300 BC - 300 AD)

Region: Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka.
Three Kingdoms: Chera (Kerala), Chola (Tamil Nadu), Pandya (Madurai).
Sangam Literature: Tolkappiyam (Tamil grammar), Silappadikaram, Manimekalai.
Trade: With Rome. Roman coins found in South India.
Society: Matriarchal in some areas.
Religion: Buddhism, Jainism, and Hinduism.

CHAPTER 7: GUPTA EMPIRE

Part 1: What Was the Gupta Empire?

The Gupta Empire is called the "Golden Age of India". It was founded by Chandragupta I in 320 AD.

Duration: 320 AD - 550 AD.
Founder: Chandragupta I.
Greatest Ruler: Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya).
Capital: Pataliputra (modern Patna).
Last Great Ruler: Skandagupta.

Part 2: Chandragupta I (320 AD - 335 AD)

Founder: Chandragupta I.
Title: Maharajadhiraja.
Capital: Pataliputra.
Marriage: Married Kumaradevi (Licchavi princess).
Significance: Founded the Gupta Empire. Started the Gupta Era.

Part 3: Samudragupta (335 AD - 375 AD)

Title: Napoleon of India (by V.A. Smith).
Conquests: Conquered whole of North India.
Religion: Hinduism (but tolerant of all religions).
Art: Patronized art and literature.
Coins: Issued gold coins.
Significance: Expanded the Gupta Empire.

Part 4: Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) (375 AD - 415 AD)

Title: Vikramaditya.
Achievements: Golden Age of India.
Navratnas: 9 gems in his court.
Kalidasa: Famous poet. Wrote Shakuntala, Meghaduta.
Aryabhatta: Famous astronomer. Wrote Surya Siddhanta.
Varahamihira: Famous astronomer. Wrote Brihat Samhita.
Fa-Hien: Chinese traveler visited during his reign.

Part 5: Skandagupta (455 AD - 467 AD)

Title: Last great Gupta ruler.
Achievements: Fought Huns.
Significance: Defended India from Huna invasions.

Part 6: Gupta Administration

Central: King was supreme. Council of Ministers.
Provincial: Empire divided into Bhuktis (provinces) and Vishayas (districts).
Local: Village headman.
Taxation: Land tax (1/6th of produce).
Judiciary: King was the highest judge.

Part 7: Gupta Art and Culture

Literature: Kalidasa (Shakuntala, Meghaduta), Vishakhadatta (Mudrarakshasa), Shudraka (Mrichchhakatika).
Science: Aryabhatta (Surya Siddhanta, decimal system), Varahamihira (Brihat Samhita), Brahmagupta.
Art: Ajanta Caves (Buddhist paintings), Ellora Caves, Sarnath Buddha.
Temples: Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh), Bhitargaon Temple.

Part 8: Decline of Gupta Empire

Huna Invasions: Foreign invasions weakened the empire.
Feudal System: Land grants to officials made them powerful.
Weak Successors: After Skandagupta, rulers were weak.
Economic Decline: Trade declined.

By 550 AD, the Gupta Empire ended.

CHAPTER 8: POST-GUPTA PERIOD

Part 1: What Was the Post-Gupta Period?

After the Gupta Empire declined in 550 AD, India was divided into many small kingdoms. This period is called the Post-Gupta Period. It lasted from 550 AD to 750 AD.

Main kingdoms: Harshavardhana (606 AD - 647 AD), Chalukya Dynasty (6th - 12th Century AD), Pallava Dynasty (4th - 9th Century AD).

Part 2: Harshavardhana (606 AD - 647 AD)

Capital: Kannauj.
Biography: Harshacharita by Banabhatta.
Religion: Initially Shaiva, later became Buddhist.
Chinese Traveler: Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang).
Death: 647 AD.
Significance: United North India for a short time.

Part 3: Chalukya Dynasty (6th - 12th Century AD)

Founder: Pulakeshin I.
Capital: Badami (Vatapi).
Greatest Ruler: Pulakeshin II.
Achievements: Defeated Harshavardhana.
Art: Chalukya temples at Aihole, Badami, Pattadakal.
Significance: Great patrons of art and architecture.

Part 4: Pallava Dynasty (4th - 9th Century AD)

Capital: Kanchipuram.
Greatest Ruler: Narasimhavarman I.
Achievements: Defeated Pulakeshin II.
Art: Mahabalipuram (Shore Temple, Pancha Rathas).
Script: Pallava script (origin of South Indian scripts).
Significance: Great patrons of art and architecture.

Part 5: Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang)

Who: Chinese Buddhist monk and traveler.
Time: 7th Century AD.
Visited: During Harshavardhana's reign.
Book: Si-Yu-Ki (Record of the Western Regions).
Significance: Described India's society, religion, and administration.
