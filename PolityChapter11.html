Emergency Provisions

CHAPTER 11 — PART XVIII (ARTICLES 352 TO 360)

When the Nation Faces Crisis
🚨 11.1 What are Emergency Provisions?

The Emergency Provisions are contained in Part XVIII (Articles 352 to 360) of the Constitution. They are inspired by the Weimar Constitution of Germany. During an emergency, the federal structure of India becomes unitary — all power concentrates in the hands of the Central Government.

Think of it like this: normally India has a "team" structure (Centre + States). During an emergency, the Centre becomes the "captain" and takes charge of everything.

⚡ Three Types of Emergency

TypeArticleGrounds
National Emergency 352 War, External Aggression, Armed Rebellion
President's Rule (State Emergency) 356 Failure of Constitutional Machinery in a State
Financial Emergency 360 Threat to Financial Stability of India
Three Emergencies: 352 (National), 356 (President's Rule), 360 (Financial). Each has different grounds and rules.

🔥 11.2 National Emergency (Article 352)

📌 11.2.1 Grounds for Proclamation

The President can declare a National Emergency if there is:

  • ⚔️ War
  • 🎯 External Aggression
  • 🔫 Armed Rebellion — The 44th Amendment replaced "Internal Disturbance" with "Armed Rebellion"
KEY POINT: The 44th Amendment (1978) made the proclamation of National Emergency more difficult. This was done to prevent misuse of emergency powers.

🏛️ 11.2.2 Parliamentary Approval

📌 Feature💬 Detail
Approval TimeWithin 1 month from the date of proclamation.
Approval ByBoth Houses of Parliament.
Majority RequiredSpecial Majority — 2/3rd of members present and voting AND majority of total membership.
ContinuationOnce approved, it continues for 6 months at a time.
ExtensionCan be extended indefinitely (6 months at a time) with fresh parliamentary approval.
KEY POINT: If the Lok Sabha is dissolved at the time of proclamation, the Rajya Sabha must approve it within 1 month. The Lok Sabha must approve it within 30 days of its first sitting after reconstitution.
National Emergency = 1 month approval, 2/3 majority, continues 6 months at a time. If Lok Sabha is dissolved, RS approves first.
