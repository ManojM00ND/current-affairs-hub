📖 7.1 What is the Judiciary?

The Judiciary is the third organ of the government (after Legislature and Executive). Think of it like the "referee" of the country — it makes sure everyone follows the rules of the Constitution.

It is the guardian of the Constitution and the protector of Fundamental Rights. India has a single integrated judicial system — meaning there is one hierarchy of courts for both the Union and the States.

This means you don't have separate court systems for the Centre and States. There's one unified system that everyone follows.

🏛️ The Hierarchy of Courts

⚖️ Supreme Court (Apex Court)
🏛️ High Courts (State Level)
🏢 District & Session Courts (District Level)
📋 Magistrate Courts (Sub-District Level)
📜 Articles to Remember
  • Articles 124 to 147 (Part V) → deal with the Supreme Court
  • Articles 214 to 231 (Part VI) → deal with the High Courts
Judiciary = The referee of India. It makes sure laws follow the Constitution and protects your rights. Supreme Court is at the top, then High Courts, then District Courts.

⚖️ 7.2 The Supreme Court of India (Articles 124 – 147)

The Supreme Court is the highest court in India. It is the final authority on all legal matters. Whatever it decides is final — no one can appeal above it.

🛡️ 7.2.2 Independence of the Supreme Court

The Supreme Court's independence means it can make decisions without pressure from the government or anyone else. This is ensured by several protections:

  • ⏳ Fixed tenure: Judges retire at 65, not at the pleasure of the President.
  • 💰 Fixed salaries: Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (not votable by Parliament).
  • ⚖️ Removal process: Very difficult (impeachment by 2/3 majority of both Houses).
  • 🚫 No practice after retirement: Judges cannot practice in any court in India after retirement.
  • 💪 Contempt power: Can punish for contempt of court (disrespecting the court).
Independence = Judges can't be fired easily, their salary can't be cut, and they can't take private cases after retirement. This keeps them free from pressure.
⚡ 7.2.3 Jurisdiction and Powers of the Supreme Court

"Jurisdiction" = the power to hear certain types of cases. SC hears disputes between states, protects rights, hears appeals, and advises the President.

🔍 7.2.4 Judicial Review

Judicial Review is the power of the Supreme Court to examine whether laws passed by the legislature and orders issued by the executive are constitutional or not.

If a law violates the Constitution, the Supreme Court can strike it down — meaning that law becomes invalid.

📌 Aspect💬 Detail
DefinitionThe power of the Supreme Court to examine the constitutionality of legislative enactments and executive orders.
SourceArticle 13
PurposeTo protect Fundamental Rights and ensure that the government does not exceed its powers.
Basic Structure DoctrineThe Supreme Court can strike down any constitutional amendment that destroys the basic structure of the Constitution (Kesavananda Bharati Case, 1973).
🔑 Terms Explained Simply
  • Judicial Review = Court checking if laws follow the Constitution
  • Strike Down = Cancel a law
  • Constitutional = Follows the Constitution
  • Unconstitutional = Violates the Constitution
Judicial Review = The Supreme Court's power to cancel any law that violates the Constitution. This protects your rights.

🏛️ 7.3 The High Courts (Articles 214 – 231)

The High Court is the highest court at the state level. Each state has a High Court (some states share one). It supervises all lower courts within its territory.
