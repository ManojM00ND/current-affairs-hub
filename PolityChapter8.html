📖 8.1 Overview of the State Executive

Just like the Union has a President, Vice-President, and Prime Minister, each State has its own set of leaders. Think of the State Government like a "mini version" of the Central Government — but it runs only one state.

The State Executive consists of four key members:

🏛️ The Four Members of the State Executive
  • 1 The Governor — Constitutional head of the State
  • 2 The Chief Minister — Real executive head
  • 3 The State Council of Ministers — To aid and advise the Governor
  • 4 The Advocate General — Chief legal advisor to the State
IMPORTANT: Articles 153 to 167 of Part VI deal with the State Executive.
State Executive = The team that runs a state. Governor is the formal head, CM is the real head.

KEY POINT: The Governor is the nominal executive (Constitutional head). The real power lies with the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers.

⚡ Powers of the Governor

Power Type💬 Details
Executive PowersAppoints the Chief Minister and other ministers. Appoints the Advocate General, State Public Service Commission members.
Legislative PowersSummons and prorogues the State Legislature. Nominates 1/6th members to the Legislative Council (if exists). Gives assent to bills. Promulgates Ordinances (Article 213).
Financial PowersMoney Bills can be introduced only with Governor's recommendation. Annual Financial Statement (State Budget) is presented in his name.
Judicial PowersAppoints judges of the High Court. Can pardon, reprieve, respite, remit, or commute sentences (Article 161). Cannot pardon death sentence (only President can).
Emergency PowersCan recommend President's Rule (Article 356) to the President.
Governor = The formal head of a state. Appointed by President. Runs the state on paper, but real power is with CM.

🚫 8.2.3 Veto Powers of the Governor
Veto Type💬 Simple Meaning
Absolute VetoWithholds assent to a bill. Bill ends.
Suspensive VetoReturns a bill to the State Legislature for reconsideration. If passed again, Governor must give assent.
Pocket VetoKeeps the bill pending indefinitely. No time limit.
KEY POINT: The Governor cannot return a Money Bill for reconsideration. He must either give assent or withhold it (but cannot return it).
Veto = Governor saying "no" to a bill. But for Money Bills, he can't send it back.
