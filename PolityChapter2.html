📖 2.1 What Are Fundamental Rights?
🎁 Simple Meaning

Fundamental Rights = Basic rights that every Indian citizen gets just by being born in India.

Think of them like "guaranteed gifts" from the Constitution that no one can take away from you.

🔑 Terms Explained Simply

📌 Term💬 Simple Meaning
Part IIIThe section of Constitution (Articles 12–35) where these rights are written
Magna Carta of IndiaA fancy name meaning "the great charter of rights" — borrowed from England's historic document
JusticiableIf someone violates your right, you can go to court (specifically Supreme Court)
Not absoluteYou can't do anything you want — government can put reasonable limits
Reasonable restrictionsSensible limits, like you can't shout "fire!" in a crowded theater
44th Amendment (1978)A change made in 1978 that removed "Right to Property" from Fundamental Rights
You have 6 basic rights. If anyone breaks them, you can run to the Supreme Court. But you also can't misuse these rights — the government can stop you if you do something harmful to society.

🎯 2.2 The Six Fundamental Rights at a Glance

1Right to Equality

Articles 14 – 18

Everyone is equal before the law. No discrimination.

2Right to Freedom

Articles 19 – 22

You can speak, move, work, and live freely.

3Right Against Exploitation

Articles 23 – 24

No forced labor or child labor. No human trafficking.

4Freedom of Religion

Articles 25 – 28

You can follow any religion you want.

5Cultural & Educational

Articles 29 – 30

Minorities can keep their language, culture, and schools.

6Constitutional Remedies

Article 32

If your rights are broken, you can go straight to Supreme Court.

💡 MEMORY TRICK: Remember the 6 Rights as "E-F-E-R-C-C" → Equality, Freedom, Exploitation, Religion, Cultural, Constitutional.
Six rights = Six shields. Each shield protects you in a different way.

🕊️ 2.4 Right to Freedom (19 – 22) — Easy

📜 Article 19: Six Freedoms

#FreedomSimple Meaning
1Speech & ExpressionYou can say what you think (with limits like no hate speech)
2AssemblyYou can gather peacefully — but without weapons
3AssociationYou can form groups, unions, clubs
4MovementYou can travel anywhere in India
5ResidenceYou can live in any part of India
6ProfessionYou can do any job or business you want

⚠️ Earlier there were 7 freedoms. The 7th was "Right to Property" — removed in 1978.

⚖️ Article 20: Protection in Respect of Conviction

TermSimple Meaning
Ex-post facto lawYou can't be punished for something that wasn't a crime when you did it
Double jeopardyYou can't be punished twice for the same crime
Self-incriminationYou can't be forced to testify against yourself
Example: If eating ice cream becomes illegal tomorrow, you can't be punished for eating it today.

❤️ Article 21: Protection of Life & Personal Liberty

Simple meaning: No one can take away your life or freedom without following proper legal process.

"Procedure established by law" = The government must follow the correct legal steps.

🔑 Judicial Expansion — What SC Added Inside Article 21

Added RightSimple Meaning
Right to HealthYou deserve medical care
Right to FoodYou shouldn't starve
Right to WaterClean water is your right
Right to PrivacyYour personal life is private (2017 case)
Right to Clean EnvironmentYou deserve fresh air and clean surroundings
Right to LivelihoodYou have a right to earn a living
Right to Die with DignityPassive euthanasia (letting a terminally ill person die peacefully)

🎓 Article 21A: Right to Education

  • Every child aged 6 to 14 gets free and compulsory education
  • Added by 86th Amendment (2002)
  • "Compulsory" = Parents must send children to school

🛡️ Article 22: Protection Against Arrest

RightSimple Meaning
Be informed of groundsPolice must tell you why you're arrested
Consult a lawyerYou can call your lawyer
Produced before magistrate in 24 hoursPolice must bring you to a judge within 1 day
Preventive DetentionPolice can hold you before you commit a crime (for national security) — but only 3 months, then Advisory Board must approve
Judicial Expansion = When judges interpret law to include more things. Advisory Board = A group of experts who check if detention is justified. Preventive Detention = Stopping you before you do something, not after.

🛡️ 2.8 Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32)

Simple meaning: If any Fundamental Right is violated, you can go directly to the Supreme Court.

❤️ Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called it the "Heart and Soul" of the Constitution.

The Supreme Court can issue 5 Writs (special orders).

📜 The 5 Writs — Easy Explanation

WritLatin MeaningSimple Meaning
Habeas Corpus"To have the body"Court orders police to bring a detained person before it
Mandamus"We command"Court orders a public official to do their duty
Prohibition"To forbid"Court stops a lower court from going beyond its power
Certiorari"To be certified"Court transfers a case from lower to higher court
Quo Warranto"By what authority"Court asks: "What right do you have to hold this post?"

Writ = A formal written order from a court.
