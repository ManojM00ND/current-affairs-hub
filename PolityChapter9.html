📖 9.1 What is Local Self-Government?

Local Self-Government refers to the system where local bodies (at the village, block, district, and city level) are given the power to manage their own affairs. Think of it like a "mini government" at the very bottom level — where people solve their own local problems.

It is the third tier of government in India (after Union and State). This means India has 3 levels: Central Government → State Government → Local Government.

🏘️ Two Types of Local Self-Government

  • 🌾 Rural Local Government — Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)
  • 🏙️ Urban Local Government — Municipalities
CONSTITUTIONAL BASIS: 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts, 1992 gave constitutional status to local bodies.
Local Self-Government = People at the village/city level governing themselves. It's the third tier of Indian democracy.

📅 9.2 Historical Background of Panchayati Raj

Panchayati Raj did not appear overnight — it evolved over decades through various committees and recommendations. Let's walk through the timeline:

1957
Balwant Rai Mehta Committee recommended a three-tier Panchayati Raj system.
1959
Rajasthan was the first state to implement Panchayati Raj (1st October 1959).
1977
Ashok Mehta Committee recommended a two-tier system.
1986
L.M. Singhvi Committee recommended constitutional status for Panchayats.
1992
73rd Constitutional Amendment Act passed.
1993
73rd Amendment came into force (24th April 1993).
Panchayati Raj evolved over 36 years (1957–1993) through committees. Rajasthan was the first state to implement it.
